Testing & Analysis

Force Analysis

Analysis: The force vs. time graph shows that over 8 seconds, the force required to compress the robot ranges between 60 to 80 newtons. Converting the units of newtons to kg/cm, the forced required is approximately 6.12 kg/cm to 8.16 kg/cm. After attaining the requried forces in kg/cm, we determined that the motor/winding system needs to withstand at least 9 kg/cm, which we used to create a gearbox to increase the torque on our motor.
Analysis: Other trials of our force vs. time graph showed similar results, which confirmed our need for a gearbox system to increase the torque on our motor, resulting in the compression required to launch the robot.

Previous Iterations

Iteration notes: One of our very first iterations of the robot. The carbon fiber strips and general idea for the mechanics have laregly stayed the same. However, we've updated our self-righting system to be more mechnical and practical, while increasing the overal complexity of the robot.

Objectives

Performance

Continuously jumps 3-10 feet on Earth, simulating up to 60ft lunar leaps on the moon.

Intelligence

Autonomous operation, wireless image transmission, and self-resetting. All allow for the operator to set the robot down, step away and capture a wide-view real time video of the lunar surface.

Durability

Designed for repeated lunar impacts, extreme temperature variations, and operations in harsh lunar dust.

Summary of the electronics for the Robot:

We created a program, using the Waveshare IMU, to check the orientation of the board before and after launch, ensuring the robot is upright and ready for launch. The board checks orientation before winding begins and immediately before the final wind and button initation. If orientation isn't correct, an LED signals an error and launch is prevented to protect the robot.

Summary of the mechanics for the Robot:

We used carbon fiber strips and springs to attach the central hub. A motor spools fishing line, compressing the robot. Then, when a release switch triggers, the stored energy in the springs and carbon fiber launch the robot; rods extend when compressed to help reorient the chassis to upright prior to jumping.

Notable Abilities

Spring Mechanism

Six springs indiivdually attached to the ineriror of the carbon fiber, all connecting back to a central disk that ensures equal force distribution.

Self-Righting

Several righting rods attached to the exterior of the carbon fiber that push agains the ground and right the robot when fully compressed.

Gear Box

Printed gear box with a 20:1 gear ratio, significantly increasing the torque on our motor.

Wireless Control

Completely operates from a driver board embedded in the top of the robot. Image is transmitted via bluetooth or a local internet server, which allows seamless video capture.

Impact Resistance

Rigid PLA that can withstand high impacts on rough surfaces, which would have more than enough strength for the lunar surface.
